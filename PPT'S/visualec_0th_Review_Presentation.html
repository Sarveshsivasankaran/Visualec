Problem Statement
PS · Problem Statement

Most rooms don't actually know if anyone's in them.

Walk into most classrooms, offices, or labs and you'll notice the same thing: every light and fan in the room is on, even if only two or three people are actually sitting there. Switches are either flipped once at the start of the day and forgotten, or run on a timer that has no idea whether the room emptied out an hour ago. Nobody's being careless — it's just that keeping track of who's where, in real time, isn't something a person can realistically do on top of everything else.

A whole room gets lit up for whoever's occupying one corner of it
Timers and manual switches don't know what's happening in the room right now
Expecting someone to walk around switching things off zone by zone isn't realistic
There's usually no easy way to even see how much of that usage was actually needed

Abstract
Abstract

Teaching a camera to notice who's actually in the room.

Visualec is my attempt at cutting down the electricity that gets wasted in classrooms, offices, labs, and other shared spaces we all use every day. It works off a laptop's camera: the live feed is split into a few virtual zones, and each zone is tied to a real appliance — a light, a fan, a prototype bulb. A lightweight AI model watches the feed and figures out which zones actually have someone in them. From there it's simple — occupied zones get switched on, and once a zone has been empty for a bit, it switches back off on its own. I also built a dashboard so I (or anyone else) can watch it happen live, tweak the zones, and get a rough sense of how much energy it's actually saving.

Sense

Camera & zones

Runs on Python, OpenCV, and a YOLO-style detector — the laptop watches the feed and works out which zone each person is standing in.

Act

ESP32-S3 & relays

Whatever the laptop decides gets sent over Wi-Fi to an ESP32-S3, which flips the relays for whichever appliances actually need to turn on.

Watch

The dashboard

A web page showing the live feed, which zones are occupied, relay status, and a running estimate of usage and savings — plus a manual override.

Keep it steady

Delays & modes

Small delays stop things flicking on and off if someone just walks through, with automatic, manual, hybrid, and emergency operating modes.

Objective
Objective

Let the room manage itself, so people don't have to.

What I'm really going for is something affordable, adaptive, and scalable — nothing exotic, just parts anyone could get their hands on. I don't want anyone to have to remember to switch anything off, or think about it at all. If the AI, the camera, and the microcontroller are doing their job, power gets used because someone's actually there — not because a clock said so, or because a switch was flipped that morning and never touched again.

AffordableJust a laptop webcam, an ESP32-S3, and relay modulesNothing here needs specialised or expensive hardware
AdaptiveFollows people, not a clockZones switch on and off on their own as people come and go
ScalableSame idea, bigger setupRoom for more cameras, more rooms, and eventually building-wide analytics
